# RETRO — 2026-08-31 · Stage 0f · Corpus scale and diversity profile

**Mode:** LOOPED · ~2 h against a 4–8 h estimate · stop condition met, not exhausted.

## What worked

**Probing the inputs before writing PLAN.md paid for itself three times.** Twenty minutes
of `head`/`md5sum`/value-counts before planning produced: the UHGG answer outright
(byte-identical md5), the discovery that the stage-0a *cache* keeps eleven columns its
*parquet* dropped (so §3.1–§3.4 needed no re-read), and — most valuable — the discovery
that `taxonomy.taxonomy_system == "ncbi"` records have phylum through family null. That
last one reshaped the whole of §3.1. Had I found it in Round 3 I would have had a plan
built on a coverage assumption that was false.

**Naming the single genuinely-absent field in PLAN.md before re-reading 79 GB.** The RT
residues were the only thing the cache lacked. Declaring that in advance, and making the
pass maximally retentive when it happened, turned "should I re-read?" from a judgement
call into a recorded decision. The pass took 116 s.

**Measuring unconditionally in §3.6 was the difference between a number and a result.**
Scoring all 501,561 proteins rather than filtering to retrons first is what makes 52.81 %
mean anything: the same instrument at the same thresholds recognised **zero** of 415,676
non-retron proteins. Filtering first would have produced 52.81 % with no way to know
whether the test could fail. EVIDENCE_STANDARDS §3 and §6 earned their place here.

## What I got wrong

**I asserted "9 of 29" from eyeballing a table, then computed 17.** I wrote a FINDINGS
sentence about non-separating HMM thresholds by scanning the calibration file visually
instead of running `awk`. The real number is 17 of 29 — a majority, not a minority, which
changes how the whole §3.6 proxy reads. Caught it on a verification pass, but only because
I went back to check. **Rule: any count that lands in prose gets computed, even a count of
29 rows.**

**I let a 29-model HMM bundle serialise an 11-minute batch** before noticing that no
statistic used it. I globbed `*.hmm` without asking what each file was. Checking
`grep -c "^HMMER3"` on the inputs would have cost five seconds.

**Two crashes that were really the same mistake: not checking what a merge would collide
on.** `family` is both a taxonomic rank and an RT family label, and the lineage join would
have produced `family_x`/`family_y` and silently shifted every per-family count. A
`KeyError` caught it — but a `KeyError` is luck, not a control. The same carelessness
produced a hand-rolled newick counter that returned 0 and asserted.

## What to fix in the specs

1. **WORKING_AGREEMENT § Editing & debugging** should say: *before any merge, list the
   column names both sides share and rename the collisions explicitly.* A join whose key
   collides with a data column does not fail — it renames, and every downstream count is
   quietly wrong. This one only failed because the collided name was also a lookup key.

2. **EVIDENCE_STANDARDS §1** covers validating an artefact by content. It should extend to
   prose: *a number that appears in a sentence is an artefact and is computed, not read
   off a screen.* Both my errors this session were numbers I looked at rather than counted.

3. **A third provenance class is missing from the inheritance rule.** WORKING_AGREEMENT
   § Inheriting artifacts names RE-DERIVED / BLIND-CONFIRMED / [UNVERIFIED] and the
   coordinator's framing was "upstream download → source data, or V3/V4 session →
   [UNVERIFIED]". `taxonomy_lookup.tsv` is neither: it is an **upstream pipeline input**,
   same class as the corpus itself. That class needs naming, because its correct grade is
   not obvious — it *looks* like source data, and it is in fact the instrument that
   assigned the property one would use it to measure.

## What surprised me

- **Taxonomic breadth is completely invariant to the dedup threshold.** 200 phyla and
  16,992 genera at every one of the five levels, while the protein unit falls 58 %. I
  expected breadth to erode with clustering and it does not move at all.
- **1,000 proteins — 0.20 % of the unique set — hold 54.11 % of all loci.**
- **117 loci where two databases disagree on the domain** of the same genomic locus.
- **`GCA_011045455.1_test`** — a `_test`-suffixed identifier in a production corpus of
  3.36 M records, found only because the resolution step reported its one failure instead
  of dropping it.
- **The corpus is deep at both ends of the lineage and hollow in the middle** — ~99–100 %
  at domain/genus/species, ~42–45 % at phylum-through-family. Not a MAG artefact; the NCBI
  assembly summaries simply carry no lineage.
