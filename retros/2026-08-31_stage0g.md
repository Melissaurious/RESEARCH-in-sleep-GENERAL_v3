# Retro — 2026-08-31 · Stage 0g (RETRON-DB V5) · Synthesis and research brief draft

Read five stages together, wrote `SYNTHESIS.md`, drafted the research brief the project
has never had, and listed what the brief's falsifier would need. ≈1 h 20 min against a
2–4 h estimate. Both gates green; nothing computed, nothing committed.

**Headline:** two of the five input stages had not finished, and the two things they were
going to supply — the circularity grades and the V4 claim dossier — are the two things the
launcher's own deliverable spec assumes exist.

---

## 1. What worked

**Surveying `STATUS.md` before reading anything else.** The launcher gives one rule for
this — *a stage without `STATUS.md` is incomplete* — and applying it first, in one `ls`,
turned an open-ended reading task into a bounded one with a declared consequence per
section. Had I read 0f's eight tables first and *then* noticed the missing `STATUS.md`, I
would have been holding numbers I was no longer allowed to treat as established, which is
a much harder position to write out of than one where the marker was attached at intake.

**Recounting instead of transcribing.** Retro 0b's lesson — *re-derive counts from the
artifact at every stage boundary* — turned into `03_dead.py`, which recomputes all eight
checkable V4 figures from `V4_INVENTORY.tsv` and prints a disagreement list. It found zero
disagreements. **That null result is what makes "5,708 of 7,746" quotable without hedging**,
and it cost twenty lines. A synthesis stage that copies numbers out of prose has no way to
say anything about them; one that recounts can say the counts hold.

**Table-lookup specs with a fail-loud selector.** Every §2.1 row is
`(table, {column: value}, column)`, and `pick()` raises unless exactly one row matches. The
values in `SYNTHESIS.md` are therefore read off disk rather than typed by me. When I
mis-specified a selector for `q1_redundancy.tsv` it raised immediately instead of silently
returning the first of two matches. **In a stage whose whole contract is "compute nothing",
the way to keep that promise is to make transcription impossible, not to be careful.**

**Writing the promotion lint as a second guard rather than a `grep`.** The plan said "grep
for established/confirmed/verified". Building it into `99_lint_adjectives.py` with an
enumerated exemption list did something a grep would not: it made each exemption a visible
line in a file rather than a decision I made once at the terminal and forgot. It fired five
times and **caught one real error** (§2).

**Recording an alternative that fails the launcher's own test.** `BRIEF_ALTERNATIVES.md`
carries a fourth question the plan did not name — what redundancy does to published retron
statistics — specifically to record that it *cannot return a negative* and so fails
launcher §3.1. A file of rejected options is more useful when it shows the rejection rule
operating than when it lists only near-misses.

---

## 2. What I got wrong

**The brief said the retron detectors were "validated" on 1,928 sequences. They were
calibrated on them, which is the opposite, and it was the sentence the whole brief is built
on.** Stage 0c's finding is that every threshold in this lineage is fitted to the set it is
scored against; writing "validated" imports exactly the assumption the brief exists to
test. I wrote it in the paragraph titled *"Why this is answerable now"*, i.e. in the place
I was being most careful.

The lint caught it, and the reason the lint caught it is worth separating from luck:
`validated` was on the list because it is a **status word**, not because I anticipated this
error. **A guard aimed at a category of mistake catches instances you did not foresee; a
guard aimed at a specific mistake catches only that one.** That is the argument for keeping
these lists broad and the exemptions enumerated.

**I ran the lint at the end, as a gate.** It rewrote four sentences after the fact. Had it
existed while I drafted §2.1, it would have shaped them instead. Stage 0a's retro makes the
same point about drafting `NOT_COMPUTABLE.md` early — *seeing an item written down as a
defeat is what prompts the check that resolves it* — and it generalises: **a check that runs
while you write is a different instrument from the same check run at the end.** Next time
the lint goes in before the first deliverable, not after the last.

**I left a dead function in `01_established.py` and only noticed on re-reading.** A
half-refactored `build()` with a placeholder line that could never be taken (`dep_col =
blind if False else dep`). It was removed before the script ran, but it was written, and it
is the shape of thing that survives into a committed script when the stage is in a hurry.

**I nearly summed the dead-artifact categories.** 5,708 + 3 + 38 = 5,749 is wrong: all 38
zero-byte artifacts and one of the three `VOID` ones already carry
`producing_script = ABSENT`. The union is **5,710**. I computed the union rather than the
sum only because the launcher's §2.3 phrasing lists three categories and I wanted the
overlap before writing "and". **Overlapping categories presented as a list invite addition;
state the union or state the overlaps.**

## 3. The structural observation — a launcher assuming outputs that do not exist

This is the finding worth promoting, and it is not about this stage's content.

Launcher §2.1 says every established quantity carries *"unit, denominator, and circularity
grade **from `CANDIDATE_STATISTICS.tsv`**"*. That file is stage 0f's §4 deliverable. Stage
0f did not write it. Likewise §1 admits stage 0e's `CLAIM_DOSSIER.tsv` *"if complete"* — it
is not — and §2.3's *"any V4 claim resting on these cannot be carried forward"* is a
sentence only 0e could have made checkable.

So a stage explicitly designed to tolerate incomplete inputs (*"Do not wait"*) still had two
of its **deliverable specifications** written against outputs that did not arrive. The
tolerance was designed at the level of *sections* and needed to be at the level of
*columns*.

**What I did:** wrote `NOT_GRADED_BY_A_COMPLETED_STAGE` in the column and stated the gap at
the head of `SYNTHESIS.md`, rather than grading circularity myself. Grading it would have
been the most natural thing in the world — I had read every method — and it would have put
a judgment with no producing stage behind it into a table that reads as assembled fact.

**What would have been better:** the launcher chain should say, for each deliverable column,
which stage owns it, so a missing owner produces an explicit `OWNER_STAGE_INCOMPLETE` token
by construction instead of by a session's restraint. **A tolerance that depends on the
session choosing not to fill a gap is not a control.** That is the same shape as the stage-0a
finding that a read boundary drawn by path cannot make a stage blind: both are mechanisms
that only work if someone also behaves.

## 4. What surprised me

**Ten of the twelve `INDEPENDENT` literature claims are not about a retron call.** They are
about reverse transcriptases in general, about tree and alignment methods, or about
annotation error as a class; the two tool-specific ones are stated limitations. Stage 0c
reports the 12/18 split as a count, and it reads as reassuring until you read what the 12
are about. **A grade distribution is not the same as an answer to "is there an independent
check of the thing I care about", and the two look identical in a table.** I recorded it in
`SYNTHESIS.md` §2.2 as a property of what stage 0c found, without inference.

**Reading three completed stages together produced one observation none of them could
make.** Stage 0c graded the instrument's provenance; stage 0f identified the reference set
exactly; stage 0a measured the labelled population. Only together do they give the two
numbers `EVIDENCE_STANDARDS` §4's scale ceiling has always been about — 1,928 and 78,287 —
as measurements rather than assertions. That is the entire case for this stage existing, and
it appeared only after all three tables were open at once.

**Stage 0f's §3.6a is a complete, provenance-carrying result inside an incomplete stage.**
Eight tables, each with a `.prov.json`, a declared normalisation ladder, and a POST-HOC
level honestly labelled everywhere it appears. The stage is incomplete; that one section is
not. The `STATUS.md` rule is right as a default and it does cost something here, which is
why the marker is `PROVISIONAL` and travels with each number rather than the number being
dropped.

# Revision 2 — appended 2026-08-31T23:18+03:00, after stages 0e and 0f finished

*Appended, not edited. Everything above is the record as written when both stages were
incomplete, including the §3 observation this section partly closes.*

Stages 0f (22:03) and 0e (22:33) landed. All six deliverables were rebuilt against their
outputs, ~1 h. **And one claim was withdrawn from the brief during the rebuild, on the
coordinator's flag.** That is the part of this round worth keeping.

## 1 · The withdrawal, and what it says about where my discipline stopped

The coordinator flagged that two claims about stage 0f §3.6 had reached the brief **from a
summary line, not from the artefact**, and asked for three things to be read off the tables,
their `.prov.json` and the producing code. Two came back determined. One did not:

- **Determined.** "Recognised" = full-sequence bit score from `hmmsearch --tblout` against
  the per-type cut read verbatim from the shipped `calibration_thresholds.tsv`.
- **Determined.** All 29 shipped thresholds were applied as shipped, **including the 17
  whose `threshold_separates` flag is `False`** — the flag is a column and there is no
  branch on it in the scoring path.
- ⚠️ **Undetermined.** Whether all 415,676 non-retron proteins were scored.
  `r10_hmm_per_type.tsv` records `n_proteins_scored` per type at **195,743–427,910** and
  **no type reaches 501,561**; `10_hmm_run.sh` passes no `-E`, `--incE`, `-T` or
  `--cut_ga`, so HMMER's default reporting threshold decided which proteins are in a table
  at all — and that threshold is recorded in neither artefact, neither `.prov.json`, nor
  `FINDINGS.md`.

**The failure is precisely locatable, and it is not "I was careless".** Round 1's design
rule was that every §2.1 quantity is a `(table, selector, column)` lookup with a fail-loud
`pick()`. I built that, and it worked — 53 rows, no transcription possible. **I then wrote
§3 and §4 of the brief by reading stage 0f's `FINDINGS.md` prose.** The guard covered the
table and stopped at the narrative, which is exactly where a number does the most work: the
52.807/0.00 pair was not decoration, it was one arm of the bracket justifying the 20 pp
margin.

**The general lesson.** A mechanism that makes transcription impossible in one file does not
make it impossible in the file that cites that file. I had the right control and drew its
boundary at the artifact rather than at the claim. This is the same shape as stage 0a's
finding that *a read boundary drawn by path cannot make a stage blind*, and stage 0d's that
*tag-stripping is a mechanism, not a guarantee*: in all three the control is sound and its
**scope** is the defect.

**The cheap fix, and it is mechanical.** Require every figure appearing in
`RESEARCH_BRIEF_DRAFT.md` to resolve to a row of `ESTABLISHED_QUANTITIES.tsv`. That is a
grep-and-join over two files this stage already writes, it would have fired on 52.807 before
the coordinator did, and it generalises to any stage whose prose cites its own tables.

**What I did with the finding, since the instruction was explicit — removed, not qualified.**
Out of brief §3 and §4; the margin reverts to the single point it had at revision 1.
`SYNTHESIS.md` §3.2 — which paired the two Δ values and was my own interpretation — is
**withdrawn in place** with the reason rather than deleted. The figures stay in §2.1i and in
the table, because that is the record of what stage 0f produced, with the gap travelling on
every affected row and a note that they are used nowhere in the brief.

**What I would have got wrong without the instruction.** My instinct was to keep the pair
and qualify it — the inference that a protein scoring 245–320 bits would certainly clear a
default reporting threshold is probably right. **But "probably right from an unrecorded
parameter" is an inference, not a reading**, and a brief that carries one is a brief whose
reader cannot tell which of its numbers are which. The instruction was better than my
instinct and the reason is worth writing down.

- **§2.3 gained the claim view it could not have.** 423 V4 claims: **40** cite `VOID`,

## 3 · The gate that caught the other defect

`check_prov.py` failed with 10 × `INPUT_MISSING`. The new 0f rows carry a composite pointer
in the `artifact` column (`CANDIDATE_STATISTICS.tsv -> <table>`), and that column was passed
straight through as the provenance input list — composite strings are not paths, and
`CANDIDATE_STATISTICS`'s own `artifact` column is **stage-relative** where mine are
project-relative. Two bugs, one symptom, both invisible to the eye and both fatal to a
provenance record. Fixed by resolving to real paths and asserting existence before hashing.

**The pattern from the first half of this retro held again**: every defect this round was
found by a mechanism that checked its own output — the prov gate that does not trust the
column, the lint that does not trust the prose. The one defect a mechanism did **not** catch
is the one the coordinator caught, and the reason is that no mechanism covered that step.

2. **Promote the promotion lint** into a shared tool, with its exemption list enumerated per
   stage. It has now caught a substantive error in each of two rounds — both times on

# Revision 3 — appended 2026-08-31T23:55+03:00

*Appended, not edited.*

Stage 0f answered both open items as addenda with their own provenance. The coordinator
relayed a third finding from another session: the Mestre reference sequences are on disk.

## 1 · The lesson from revision 2 generalises in the direction I did not expect

Revision 2's lesson was written about a claim that turned out to be **unsupported**: I had
taken a figure from a summary line, and reading the artefact removed it. The natural reading
of that lesson is "check claims that sound too good".

Both revision-3 findings pointed the **other** way — one restored a claim I had withdrawn,
the other lifted a blocker I had recorded as hard. **Both still needed checking, and both
were wrong in their summaries:**

- The HMM denominator finding was **right**, and checking it was still what made it usable:
  the addendum's prose says the numbers do not change, and only `r13_*.tsv` shows *why* —
  `n_submitted` = 501,561 on all 29 models and a worst-case E of 6.6 × 10⁻⁵¹ against a cut
  of 10. Restoring on the prose would have restored a claim I could not defend.
- The residues finding was **materially overstated**. "1,926 sequences, joinable to his
  tree" became, on reading: **1,813 exact reference members**, 105–112 `|rescued` PATRIC
  re-resolutions at a different genome version *and* peg number, and a newick whose tips are
  accessions rather than `terminal_N`. And `ground_truth_metadata.json`, hoped to be "the
  answer key clade-recovery scoring has been missing", has **`ncRNA: null` in all 1,925
  files and no clade field at all** — it is a residue source, not a label source.

**The sharpened rule: a summary is not a reading regardless of which direction it points —
and the pull to skip the check is stronger when the news is welcome.** I felt that pull. The
withdrawn claim was mine, I wanted it back, and the temptation was to restore on the strength
of "0f says it's fine".

## 2 · Two things the check found that neither summary contained

**The two residue artefacts agree with each other.** 1,813 from a JSON tree and 1,814 from a
FASTA, built by different sessions for different purposes. Neither summary mentioned the
other artefact. **That independent agreement is the actual reason to trust the figure**, and
it only exists because I checked both instead of the one I was pointed at.

**Stage 0f's miss has a larger cause than its own addendum names.** 0f wrote that it
"searched for a file format instead of for the content" — true, and it found 170 in a CSV
column. But its sweep covered 819 FASTA-like files **outside `ARIS_OUTPUT`**, and every large
artefact is **inside** it. **The scope exclusion, not the format assumption, is what cost it
1,643 sequences.** A stage whose blind boundary forbids reading V4 analysis output will
naturally exclude `ARIS_OUTPUT` from a sweep — and then cannot see data that happens to live
there. Worth naming: *a read boundary drawn for one purpose silently becomes a search
boundary for another.* That is the third instance of this shape in this project's retros.

## 3 · What I got right, and it was one decision

**Withdrawing rather than qualifying at revision 2.** My instinct then was to keep the pair
with a caveat, and the coordinator's instruction overrode it. Revision 3 shows why the
instruction was better: because the claim was **out**, restoring it required a measurement,
and the measurement arrived. Had it been in-with-a-caveat, nothing would have forced the
measurement and the caveat would have quietly aged into the record. **Removal creates
pressure to resolve; qualification releases it.**

The round trip is now legible in four places — `SYNTHESIS.md` §2.1i and §3.2, the brief's §3,
and the affected rows' notes — and that is deliberate. A figure that went out and came back
is in a different evidential state from one never questioned, and a reader should be able to
see which.

# Revision 4 — appended 2026-09-01 · the spec change landed, session closed

*Appended, not edited. This closes stage 0g.*

## What landed

`specs/EVIDENCE_STANDARDS.md` **§1c — Grade the CLAIM, not the number**, placed after §1b
so the three read as one progression: validate the artefact rather than its existence,
enforce blindness by content rather than by path, **grade the claim rather than the
numeral**. All three are the same defect at a different scope — a control that is sound but
attached to the wrong object.

The rule, in the coordinator's words: *a quantity read from a table that does not contain
it, or read by eye rather than computed, is `INFERRED` even when a table was open. Record
the grade per claim, not per figure.* Written up with a three-grade rubric
(`MEASURED` / `DERIVED` / `INFERRED`), and extended to the two things this session actually
tripped on beyond counts — **negatives** and **unexamined tool defaults**.

One line was added to the §8 checklist as well, because every other rule in the file is
mirrored there and leaving it out would make the checklist the only place §1c does not
exist. **That was my call, not an instruction** — easy to revert if the checklist is meant
to stay as it was.

## Why the wording matters more than it looks

**"Per claim, not per figure" is the whole rule.** Every number in the withdrawn sentence
was correct. `52.807` was correct. `0.00` was correct. `415,676` was correct. The sentence
was not, because the denominator came from a different table than the numerator and nothing
recorded that. A grading scheme that certifies figures would have passed all four and
missed the claim. **A figure has no evidential state of its own** — it acquires one only
inside a sentence, with a unit, a denominator and a scope, and that is the unit of grading.

The three failures this session produced map cleanly onto the three `INFERRED` triggers,
which is the argument that the rubric is the right shape:

| what happened | trigger |
|---|---|
| the 52.807/0.00 pair taken from stage 0f's prose while `r10_*.tsv` sat unopened | read by eye rather than computed |
| *"the reference residues are not on disk"* — from a sweep that excluded `ARIS_OUTPUT` | a negative whose scope did not cover the claim |
| HMMER's `-E 10.0` treated as if it were recorded | an unexamined tool default |

## The one clause I would defend hardest

**"Do NOT rescue a claim by qualifying it."** That is the least obvious line in §1c and the
one this session earned. My instinct at revision 2 was to keep the pair with a caveat, and
the instruction to remove it was better — because removal is what forced the measurement
that restored it at revision 3. A qualified claim would have sat in the brief with a hedge
attached and nothing would ever have compelled `r13_*.tsv` to exist. **Removal creates
pressure to resolve; qualification releases it.**

## Session close

Stage 0g halts at revision 3: six launcher deliverables plus two verification files, five
tables with provenance, `check_prov.py` PASS with zero warnings, both lints at 0 hits over
8 deliverables. 53 established quantities · 32 `unproven` literature claims · 423 V4 paper
claims dossiered · 68 open items, 64 `OPEN`. `RESEARCH_BRIEF_DRAFT.md` remains in the stage
directory with its date at `<COORDINATOR: date>`; promotion and `/kill-argument` are the
coordinator's. No compute queued, nothing running, nothing scheduled, nothing committed by
this stage.

# Revision 5 — appended 2026-09-01 · §1c caught its author

*Appended, not edited. This closes stage 0g for real.*

## The rule I wrote one round too late

Revision 4 added `EVIDENCE_STANDARDS` §1c — *grade the claim, not the number* — with three
`INFERRED` triggers, one of them **"a quantity read from a table that does not contain it"**.
Revision 5 is that trigger firing on the sentence I wrote in revision 3.

I reported **"the usable count is 1,813, not 1,926"**. The intersection is exact and I
computed it with a fail-loud selector against the artefact. What was wrong is that I measured
*how many FASTA headers carry an accession matching a reference accession* and reported it as
*how many reference residues are available*. **The FASTA does not contain availability. It
contains headers.** The join key is `terminal_N` → `Node` in the systematic-prediction CSV,
and on that key it is **1,926 of 1,926, every one carrying a clade label**. The 1,814
accession agreement is the **control that validates the join**, not a shortfall in it.

## Why every gate passed anyway

This is the part worth keeping. The claim survived:

- a fail-loud selector — it matched exactly what I asked for;
- `check_prov.py` — the artefact exists, is hashed, and is on disk;
- both lints — no adjective, no promotion word;
- and revision 3's own artefact-first discipline, which I *did* apply. I read the FASTA
  rather than trusting the summary. I read the right file.

**Every control this stage runs checks that a figure came from a file. None checks that the
file answers the question.** I applied artefact-first to the *finding* and never to the
*question* — I did not ask which field the two files were meant to join on, and reading the
right artefact with the wrong key yields an exact number answering a question nobody asked.

That is precisely the gap §1c names, and I could not see it in my own sentence while writing
the rule for it. **The gap between stating a rule and being subject to it is not knowledge,
it is a control** — §1c will only work when something other than the author's attention
enforces it, the way `pick()` enforces the selector.

## What I would add to §1c if asked (not added unasked)
